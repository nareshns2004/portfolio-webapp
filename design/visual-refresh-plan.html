Visual refresh: the “datasheet” direction

The content now carries the site. This plan changes how it looks: away from the generic dark-terminal template and toward something that reads like a well-made engineering datasheet or paper figure. Nothing ships until you approve it.

This page is itself styled in the proposed system. Use the toggle (top right) to see dark mode. With reduced motion turned on, the hero animation becomes a static three-panel storyboard.

Now: dark terminal

Proposed: datasheet
GPU cluster networking · training infra
Warm paper, ink, one signal color per story
Reads as “engineer who documents things properly”. Diagrams look like figures in a paper, which is what they are.

Principles

  • Color means something. Signal blue = reliability and links. Copper = efficiency. Fault red = failures only. No decorative gradients or glow.
  • Diagrams are the visual identity. The ring, the fabric and the timelines look like figures: thin ink strokes, mono labels, hatching instead of glow for “waiting”.
  • Type does the hierarchy. One sans family for everything you read, its mono sibling for anything you’d copy or measure.
  • Light by default, honest dark mode. Dark follows the system setting (with a toggle) and uses deep slate, not terminal black.
1 · Palette

Six named colors

Each has a light and dark value. Contrast is measured against Paper in the same theme; all text uses pass WCAG AA (≥ 4.5:1).

Widget states use the same six: running = Signal, waiting = Graphite hatching, failed = Fault, done = solid Ink. Amber and green are dropped. Fewer colors make each one mean more.

2 · Typography

IBM Plex Sans + IBM Plex Mono

One superfamily, two cuts. Plex was drawn for engineering documentation: legible at small sizes, with a mono whose numerals line up in tables and widgets. It replaces Syne (display), JetBrains Mono (UI) and the system sans (prose), so we go from three families to two.

3 · The hero moment

A ring loses a link, and the goodput bar shows what it costs

A figure, not a decoration. Eight GPUs run ring all-reduce and the timeline below fills with blue (productive time). Then link 2→3 fails: the stall spreads one hop at a time (hatching), every rank times out, the job restores from a checkpoint onto a spare, and the ring resumes. The bar keeps the red and grey segments. That is the headline, drawn.
